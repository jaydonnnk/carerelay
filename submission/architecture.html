Fictional research demonstration. Not clinical advice. Not a medical device.

CareRelay: where the model reads words, and where code decides

CareRelay addresses the care-navigation part of Challenge 1, helping older adults understand a supplied care plan, identify barriers and see whether help has actually been arranged. It does not assess symptoms or choose care.

The path of one fictional episode, as built

  1. Fixed script

    Fictional supplied plan

    Action, deadline, who acts and fallback come from a fixed fixture. No model writes them.

  2. Patient

    Confirmed patient words

    The plan is folded away. The patient says it back, reviews the words and confirms them before any check.

  3. Tencent ADP

    Phrase extraction

    One call, 15-second cap, no retry, schema v2. It copies the phrases for action, time and who acts, or marks them unclear, or refuses.

  4. CareRelay code

    Validation and comparison

    Code rejects a malformed reply, locates each phrase in the saved words and compares it with the stored plan.

  5. CareRelay code

    Patient next step

    Code picks the next screen: repair the time, ask what makes it hard, or stop. The plan and deadline never change here.

  6. Patient

    Permission

    Nothing is shared unless the patient agrees on its own step. Only the action and deadline would be shared.

  7. Simulated provider

    Simulated action

    A scripted local provider receives one request. Its reply arrives separately and is labelled simulated.

  8. CareRelay code

    Recorded outcome

    Request sent, proof of care and case state are separate axes. A failed request keeps the deadline and the fallback, and the case stays open.

Offline mode. ADP is off by default. Without explicit local configuration, step 3 is done by a labelled local stand-in, and the judge view says Tencent ADP was not contacted.

What Tencent ADP does and never does

ADP does

  • Read the words the patient confirmed.
  • Copy out the action, time and who acts, exactly as written.
  • Mark doubt, negation, alternatives and changed minds as unclear.
  • Refuse medical questions and injected instructions.

The model never

  • Diagnoses, chooses urgency or prescribes.
  • Sets or changes the plan, the deadline or who acts.
  • Grades whether the patient understood.
  • Grants permission, sends a request or declares care complete.
  • Gets a second try when its reply is wrong.

Clinical functions this build does not have

  • Clinical symptom assessment.
  • Warning-sign (red-flag) detection.
  • Validated self-care guidance.
  • Dynamic clinical reassessment over time.
  • Fairness validation across patient subgroups.

Refusing a clinical request is a scope guard, not evidence of calibrated clinical triage. A stopped conversation or a failed request does not mean anyone has taken over. Patient benefit, clinical safety and superiority over existing apps are unmeasured.

Possible future role, not built

Alongside HealthHub or Health Buddy Proposed. No connection exists.

A national app could supply a real, clinician-issued plan and a real booking route. CareRelay could then sit beside it as the read-back and follow-through layer: check the plan was understood, ask what stands in the way, and show plainly whether help has been arranged. No integration, data exchange, agreement or endorsement exists today.
