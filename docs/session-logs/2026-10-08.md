## Gate B ambiguity: the second scorer is not the blocker

Jaydon asked who the second scorer is, offered "Elizabeth", then said Elizabeth was
invented to probe what "second scorer" meant. **No name was recorded anywhere.** The

Investigating the ambiguity problem instead turned up findings that are all
**verified by running the scorers**, not read off the docstrings:

- **The action field cannot be scored `incorrect`.** `scoring.DIFFERENT_ACTION_FORMS`
  is empty, because the fixture's alias table keys the keyed action only. So
  `score_action_recall` returns True or raises: at runtime the label is `correct` or
  `unclassified`, never `incorrect`. `"call the doctor"` gives `unclassified`, and so
  does `"take her to the clinic"`, a natural paraphrase. The accepted set is 8 exact
  strings after `rules.normalise`.
- **The deadline list is 10 exact strings plus 3 known-wrong forms.** `"7pm"` is
  `unclassified`, not `incorrect`. So are `"tomorrow morning"`, `"before dinner"` and
  `"sometime next week"`.
- **The primary outcome's yes/no lists are hand-typed** (7 and 6 strings), which
  contradicts `scoring.py`'s own stated principle that the vocabularies are derived.
  `"I havent yet"`, `"no not yet"` and `"maybe"` all give `unclassified`.
- **A pending false-completion verdict blocks a cut rule that never reads it.**
  `pending_second_scorer` (outcomes.py 296-312) queues `FALSE_COMPLETION_FIELD`, and
  `apply_cut_rule` (390-456) refuses on any pending item at 422-427, before it
  computes. The cut rule reads `both_correct` only.

**The answer to the question he asked:** the second scorer is a patch for a handful
of odd answers. Against vocabularies of 8 and 10 exact strings she would be
hand-classifying most of the study, which turns a pre-registered rule into human
adjudication. **The blocker is the vocabularies, not the person.** No rate of
unclassified answers has been measured on real dyads; that part is a hypothesis.

Four routes were laid out: widen the vocabularies (or call the application's own
resolver, which risks flattering condition A), close the question so recall becomes
recognition, pre-register an exclusion rule for unclassified rows, or keep a second
scorer with a bounded scope and report how many rows she touched.

## Route chosen: 2, close the question (decision, not yet implemented)

Jaydon asked for one of the four routes. Chose **route 2**, on new evidence that kills
route 1 outright.

**Route 1 is dead, measured.** `compare_plan` resolves through `_resolve_id` /
`_resolve_deadline`, and both are `normalise` + **exact lookup** in the alias and
deadline tables. There is no span extraction and no paraphrase handling, so the
application's resolver has exactly the same coverage as the scorer: "take her to the
clinic" -> uncertain, "7pm" -> uncertain. It is also *narrower*: `deadline_forms` does
not contain `"6pm today"`, the string the card itself prints, so routing the scorer
through the resolver would turn the most likely verbatim answer into uncertain. Calling
it adds zero coverage and keeps only the condition-A flattery risk. Widening by hand
instead reintroduces B1.

**Route 3 does not fix anything.** Excluding unclassified rows leaves the action field
still unable to return `incorrect`, drops rows non-randomly, and n=6 cannot afford it.
**Route 4 is the residual, not the fix** - keep her, report how many rows she touched.

**Why 2:** it is the only route where the wrong-action vocabulary is *derived*
(`permitted_action_ids`) rather than hand-typed, and the only one where every response
is classifiable by construction - one of the options, or "don't know" = did not recall =
`incorrect`. Unclassified becomes unreachable on both recall fields, so K1 is satisfiable
on the half of `both_correct` that currently cannot be wrong.

**Costs stated to Jaydon:** recall becomes recognition, so the recall half of the cut
rule moves toward ceiling and the decision collapses onto median burden at n=3-6; the
result must be labelled recognition; and it is a pre-data amendment to frozen section 7
that must be recorded in section 17 before the first dyad. The bias runs *against* our
own hypothesis, which is the safe direction for a kill test.

**One dependency, unresolved:** the two distractor action ids (`call_nurse_line`,
`wait_and_monitor`) exist as ids with **no patient-facing text anywhere** in the repo.
Route 2 needs that wording authored, and it is patient copy, so it is governed by the
five copy rules and the authored/unsourced/non-clinical rule. Ordering that protects the
primary outcome: the belief question is asked first and open, so the closed recall
questions cannot cue it.
