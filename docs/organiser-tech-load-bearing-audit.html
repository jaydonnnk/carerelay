CareRelay · Gate 1 decision aid

Load-bearing is necessary. It is not enough.

Reality check · 22 September 2026 Primary-source refresh. Product claims remain unproved. Gate 1 remains open.

Corrected verdict

The one-shot scope is platform-light. The broad longitudinal reframe is also market-crowded. The remaining bet is narrower: a monitored episode with measured, fail-closed abstention that beats the fixed local baseline.

Decision frame

Four tests, none optional

P1 is an internal lens, not an organiser rule. The safe version asks for one genuinely load-bearing axis—not technical exclusivity.

P1

Load-bearing

Removing organiser tech should break a central demo behaviour, not just remove a logo.

P2

Early

Do not call follow-up, cohort monitoring or abstention itself new.

P3

Track-aligned

Dynamic reassessment and calibrated refusal are strong fits, not mandated implementations.

P4

Ambitious

Keep the monitored-episode vision; prove one narrow slice before expanding it.

Decision

Keep the vision. Shrink the proof.

The longitudinal additions are unestimated. Calling them effort-neutral would be false.

Keep

  • One application-owned multi-day episode
  • One real scheduled reassessment/resume path
  • Abstention as an explicit recorded outcome
  • Closure Contract and immutable deadline
  • Fixed card + NurseFirst comparison

Do not claim

  • WorkBuddy is technically irreplaceable
  • The organiser requires scheduler, sessions or scale
  • Cohort monitoring or abstention is new
  • A chat session is durable clinical state
  • The added scope is effort-neutral
A

Native access: real scheduled invocation, typed tool failure and resume—or stop the WorkBuddy-led claim.

B

Patient advantage: if the fixed card + NurseFirst matches recall, false-closure recognition and burden, cut PlanBack or change direction.

C

Platform advantage: remove WorkBuddy and show exactly which judged behaviour disappears.

D

Clinical integrity: no deployment claim without an authorised protocol and qualified reviewer.
