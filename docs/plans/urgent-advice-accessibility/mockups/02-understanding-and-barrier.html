The three boxes are the whole hint. They show the shape of the answer, never the answer.

“Show my plan” always works and never disappears — but using it records a cued outcome, not recall.

Hint ladder — how the prompt escalates

Each level is recorded

LevelWhat the patient seesRecorded as
H0The question alonerecall_unaided
H1Three labelled slots: What · When · Who helpsrecall_scaffolded
H2Plan card shown again — stays until the patient hides itrecall_cued
H3Plan shown; patient confirms by choosingnot_recalled
  • H0–H2 must not display the critical fields — no deadline, no action, no owner. A hint containing the answer turns a comprehension check into a reading test.
  • No timers anywhere. At H2 the card stays until the patient hides it. Nothing auto-hides, auto-advances or counts down — a five-second rule was rejected as unreadable for older adults and unusable with a screen reader.
  • H3 is honest, not failure. not_recalled routes to a human path and is reported as such.
  • Warm tone, hard content limit. Big type, plain words, no blame — but the answer stays out of the hint.
The ladder also fades — support is withdrawn as competence is shown

Vanishing cues, errorless learning, spaced retrieval

An established cognitive-rehabilitation technique, cited as prior art. Not invented here, and not a brain-training game.

EventNext comparable plan starts at
First plan, or a changed planH1 — never a blank prompt

Failure, or a cued outcomePrevious level restored
A single bad roundNo change — trends only
  • Fading is invisible. No score, no streak, no progress bar, no level ever shown to the patient.
  • What it achieves: better retention of this plan, less prompt dependence, lower caregiver burden.
  • What it does NOT claim: improved general cognition, slowed decline, screening, diagnosis, or any risk score. Meta-analyses find no far transfer from cognitive training, and the FTC fined Lumosity $2 million in 2016 for claiming otherwise.
  • Language rule: never “cognitive rot,” “brain training,” or “use it or lose it” in anything user-facing.
